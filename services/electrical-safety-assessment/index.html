A useful assessment has a stated purpose and boundary

A condition concern, renovation decision or insurer request can each call for a different assessment. Agree which equipment and areas will be reviewed, whether testing is included and what written result will be provided. Concealed wiring and inaccessible equipment may limit conclusions. A general visit should not be represented as complete certification of everything in the property.

Prepare the scope for your Fergus property

List the major load you intend to add, such as a through-floor lift in a two-storey home, and where it will stand, then note the areas you want reviewed. Collect any inspection reports, earlier ESA paperwork or insurer letters.

Ask the contractor what the review includes, whether any testing is part of it and which areas cannot be reached. Ask what written result you will receive and whether it can be shared with an insurer or lender.

What should the assessment establish?

With no stated purpose, no list of what was covered and no word on what stayed unseen, a report on a planned load gives a Fergus household nothing firm to plan around. Purpose, the equipment and areas reviewed, and the limits on what could be seen belong in writing. The existing supply and panel are recorded as either a suitable base for the new load or in need of further assessment. Concealed wiring and areas behind finished walls appear as not reviewed. Each defect found is described plainly with its location, and any recommended next step stays separate from a repair quote.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

What does a review before a new load cover and not cover?

It covers the equipment and areas agreed in advance, and only what the contractor can reach and see. It does not clear the whole property, so ask for the limits to be written down.

Can the review be shared with an insurer or a lender?

Ask the contractor, before the visit, whether the written result can be shared and what the insurer or lender requires it to say. A report written for one purpose may not satisfy another.
