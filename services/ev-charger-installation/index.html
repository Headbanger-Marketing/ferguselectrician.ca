The charger is only one part of the installation

An EV charging project includes equipment, a suitable connection and an assessment of the property supply. ESA advises using approved charging equipment, a dedicated circuit and outdoor-rated equipment where needed. The contractor should evaluate the proposed load and explain whether the supply, a load-management arrangement or another design is suitable. Do not choose a circuit from the charger advertising alone.

Prepare the scope for your Fergus property

List every new load planned this year, such as a charger, a heat pump or a cooktop, and note where each will stand. Record where the vehicle parks and how far that spot is from the panel.

Ask the contractor whether the quote assesses all of these loads together or the charger alone, and what would change if one of them is added later. Ask how the charger position suits a user who may need a shorter reach.

What should the assessment establish?

Charging is being added to a Fergus home in the same year as other new loads. Those loads are weighed together as one combined demand on the supply. Whether the present supply can take that demand, whether load management belongs in the design and whether the utility has to be told are each answered in the scope. That document also traces the dedicated circuit route, the charger's location and any outdoor rating needed, with a mounting height and cable reach that let it be used without strain. Whoever files the ESA notification is named as well.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance, and EV charging safety guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can a second charger be added later if the first is installed now?

It may be possible, but a later charger adds to the same supply. Tell the contractor about any second vehicle now, and ask the written design to say whether it allows for that addition or would need a new assessment.

Does the charger location matter for people with limited reach?

Yes. Cable weight, mounting height and the path from the parking spot all affect how easily the charger can be used. Photograph the parking position and describe who will plug in, so the contractor can plan the location.
