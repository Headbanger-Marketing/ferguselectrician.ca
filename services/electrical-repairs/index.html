A symptom is a starting point, not a diagnosis

Loss of power, flickering, recurring protective-device trips or a damaged device can have different causes. The useful information is what happened, where and when, not a guess made from equipment age. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess the installation within a defined diagnostic scope and explain the identified cause before repair work is approved.

Prepare the scope for your Fergus property

Write down which labelled device would not reset, what was running at that moment and whether any nearby item looked discoloured, buzzed or smelled odd. Photograph the shut panel door with its labels and keep every cover closed.

Ask the contractor what a diagnostic visit includes, what it leaves out and what written finding follows it. Ask who must be home and whether a staircase or locked room restricts reach.

What should the assessment establish?

The symptom of a protective device that will not reset is recorded first, with the affected circuit and the equipment in use. Testing should stay within a defined scope, and from it the contractor states whether the fault lies in the device, the wiring or something plugged in. Repair belongs after that cause is named, and the contractor then describes the parts and any finished surfaces to be opened. Where that circuit feeds a freezer or a stair light in a Fergus home, the written result tells the household how to manage without it until the repair.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

What should I do instead of retrying a device that trips again?

Leave it alone rather than trying again, because repeated resetting can hide a developing problem. Tell the contractor which breaker was involved, what was running and how often it trips.

Who needs to be home while a fault visit takes place?

Someone able to authorize access and recall the history should be there. If the panel or the affected room is awkward to reach, say so when describing the symptom so the visit can be planned around it.
