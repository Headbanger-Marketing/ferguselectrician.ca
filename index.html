Before work begins

Plan reachable controls before any wall is opened.

One home may have an open attic overhead, while a two-storey home can have little usable attic or ceiling access. There, a cable to a second-floor ceiling may run through finished walls between floors, which can change how reach, height and routes are planned.

Whatever the home, write down who will use each switch, receptacle and light, which tasks are hard today, and how that may change, so the contractor prices the controls you need rather than a standard layout.

Can switch and receptacle heights be changed during other work?

Often they can be raised or lowered when the wall is already being opened, but the route behind the finish decides how much work that is. Ask the contractor to show each new height on a sketch before the scope is agreed.

How do I describe a lighting problem on a staircase or entrance?

Say which steps or doorway are hard to see, at what time of day, and who uses them. Photograph the area and note where a switch would be easiest to reach without leaning or climbing.

Should I compare quotes that place controls in different spots?

Yes, but compare them on the same written layout. Ask each contractor to list every control position, the height used and any wall or ceiling that must be opened and repaired, so the differences are visible.

Who must agree before changes are made for someone else in the home?

The owner of the property decides, and a landlord, condominium corporation or co-owner may also need to agree. Write down who can authorize the work and who can open the door when the contractor visits.
